The Atomic Age

1950–1959. Mid-century modern, when postwar optimism turned the suburbs into a color laboratory.

Design choices

Color: I used the 1950s appliance, diner and car pastels (mint, coral, butter yellow, powder blue) on a warm parchment ground, with charcoal for outlines and text so the soft colors stay readable. Shape: the starburst and boomerang leaf are the decade's signature motifs from clocks, lamps and barware, built from simple geometry. Scale: one oversized burst and a heavy headline lead the page, while the small leaves and swatches stay quiet. Repetition and rhythm: twelve rays and fourteen leaves repeat in a steady cycle, and the leaves cycle through four colors, much like a terrazzo floor or a jukebox row. Movement and timing: the burst turns slowly (48 seconds) like a wall clock, its rays pulse in staggered delays, and the leaves bob in a 2.4 second wave, bouncy but never frantic, matching the decade's cheerful optimism.
